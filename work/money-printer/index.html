What it does

Each day an agent writes short scripts about true stories from history into a queue. The pipeline voices each script with a local text-to-speech model, finds public-domain footage on Archive.org (passed through a vision check and a blank-clip check) and public-domain photos from Wikimedia, applies a colour grade, normalises loudness to -14 LUFS, renders the Short and schedules two uploads a day at times chosen for a US evening audience. Separate analyst agents then pull channel data every day and write reports on traffic sources, audience, first-two-day views and retention.

Decisions that matter

  • Everything runs on free tools and public-domain media only.
  • No automated engagement comments, because they look like a bot.
  • New formats need my explicit approval before anything is published.
  • Any new automation must fail closed if it cannot verify which channel it is about to post to.
  • The Claude track and the Codex track (a separate vertical-drama workflow for a channel that does not exist yet) never share credentials, schedules or analytics.

Where it stands

The channel is small and not monetised. After a format change, first-two-day views dropped sharply, and I recorded the cause as unproven rather than guessing. A 16:9 long-form pilot (8.4 minutes, five stories) has been rendered but not published.

Honest limits

  • Growth is slow and the cause of the recent drop is not yet known.
  • The YouTube login token expires every 7 days until the app's consent screen is moved to production.

What I took away

Running a pipeline that posts for real teaches different lessons from a demo. The hard parts were failure handling, scheduling and knowing when the data is too thin to draw a conclusion.
